What it looks like in use

One family's record, from the booked tour to the signed contract, the bills and the sales team's commission.

A membership sale, step by step

  1. A family asks about membership

    They fill in a short form online, and the sales team has a new lead with a household record.

  2. They are booked into a tour time

    The lead picks a slot on the calendar, then gets a confirmation and a reminder message.

  3. The contract is signed on screen

    The family signs in the browser, and a PDF copy of the signed contract is saved to their record.

  4. Payments and bills run on a schedule

    The first payment is taken, and later monthly, quarterly or yearly invoices are created and sent on time.

  5. Commission is worked out for the team

    Each person's share is calculated from the contract, then checked on a payroll review screen.

01

The challenge

A membership sales operation runs a long pipeline: capture prospects, book them into scheduled sessions, confirm attendance, close a contract, collect payment, then bill recurring fees and pay commissions.

Without a single system, each step lives in a separate tool: forms, calendars, texting, payment links, accounting and payroll spreadsheets. That causes re-keying and disagreement over contract and payment status. The handover document describes all of these steps in one system with many roles involved.

02

The solution

A Laravel 12 monolith with Blade, Vue 3 and Alpine front ends. A lead form feeds scheduling, with campaigns, days, waves and time slots. Confirmation and reminder messaging follows, then contract creation with in-browser signature and PDF archiving.

Payments, recurring billing, invoices and commission calculation hang off the contract. Role-based access separates sales, management, payroll and admin views. A headless authentication API serves an external member portal.

05

Implementation notes

Engineering decisions worth knowing about.

  1. Role and permission access control

    Role and permission middleware separates sales, management, payroll and admin views.

  2. Dual authentication

    Sanctum for the single-page app and JWT for external API consumers, plus key-based API clients.

  3. Queues and scheduled commands

    Queue jobs are configured for Redis, with a database driver as fallback. 25 artisan commands cover billing, reminders and sync, about ten of them registered in the scheduler.

  4. Model observers

    Observers on leads and contracts keep derived data consistent.

  5. Integrations in service classes

    Telephony SMS, email delivery, calendar, geocoding, Slack and Zoom are each isolated behind a service.

  6. Log sanitizing

    A log processor keeps sensitive values out of logs.

  7. OpenAPI documentation

    API docs are generated with a Swagger package.

  8. Iterative schema history

    Roughly 100 migrations show how the data model evolved.

Business value

What the system enables. No numbers are claimed.

  • Keeps the whole prospect-to-payment journey in one record chain, avoiding re-keying between tools.
  • Automates recurring invoices, reminders and commission calculation that would otherwise be manual.
  • Separates access by role so sales, payroll and admins see only what they need.
  • Exposes a headless API so a member-facing site can reuse the same accounts.
