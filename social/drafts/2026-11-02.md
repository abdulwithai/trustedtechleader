## Tuesday — Supplier catalog sync: AI drafts, a person tests
Source: src/app/case-studies/supplier-catalog-sync-platform/page.tsx

### LinkedIn
Every supplier speaks a different API language.

One anonymised project we built: a catalog console for a business that resells promotional products and apparel. Each supplier has its own API, auth scheme and field names.

The usual way to onboard one is a developer reading the docs and hand-writing an integration.

The project's own notes list the pain points:
- very large catalogs
- refresh tokens that stop working after one use
- APIs that can't tell you what changed
- silent failures when a script reads the wrong field name

So we changed the order of work.

An operator uploads the supplier's documentation as a PDF. AI drafts the connector spec and field mapping. The operator tests the connection and previews real sample rows before anything is saved.

AI drafts. A person checks.

Every operator action goes to an activity trail, so a failed check shows up instead of staying silent.

It's described from the codebase. No metrics are claimed.

## Thursday — Law firms: reply fast and recover missed consults
Source: src/lib/funnel-content/law-firms.ts

### LinkedIn
A missed consultation isn't always a lost client.

It often just means the conversation stopped.

For immigration, personal injury and family law firms, the first inquiry is only half the job. The other half is what happens when the consultation doesn't happen.

A practical checklist for your intake:

1. Put every source in one place. Website forms, WhatsApp and ad leads shouldn't live in three inboxes.
2. Reply first, even briefly. Offer consult times right away, including after hours.
3. Read the attachments before the call. Notices, reports and court orders hold the details your attorney will ask for.
4. Plan for the no-show. A call and a text with a rebooking link keep it moving.
5. Measure it. Track time to first response and how many leads get followed up.

None of this replaces your intake team. It removes copying leads and chasing replies, so they can handle conversations.

This is general operations advice, not legal advice. Check the contact and consent rules in your state before automating calls or texts.

## Saturday — A security report should be a plan, not a scary PDF
Source: src/components/ai-code-audit/sections/Report.tsx

### LinkedIn
A security report nobody acts on is just a scary PDF.

When we describe our AI code security audit, we say this plainly: the report should be a plan you can act on.

Every audit includes:
- a plain-English executive summary
- severity-ranked findings with proof
- a code-level fix for each finding
- a walkthrough call
- a retest after fixes
- a production launch checklist

On the site there's a sample report with illustrative findings. One example is a service-role key shipped to the browser. The sample's fix: move it to a server-only environment variable and rotate the key.

Our view is simple. If you ship AI-generated code, you don't need more fear. You need to know what is wrong, how we proved it, and what to change first.

Proof matters, because a finding without it is an opinion. The fix matters, because a finding without it is a worry.

The sample report is illustrative, not client data.
