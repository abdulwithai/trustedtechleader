NotiPad Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 27, 2026
Developer: abdulwithai
Contact: abdulrahman.connect@outlook.com

Noti is a notes app for Windows that keeps your notes on your own PC. This policy explains, in plain language, what Noti stores, where it goes, and what you can do about it.

Summary

  • Your notes are stored on your PC. You do not need an account to use Noti.
  • The developer does not collect your notes or any usage data. Noti's source contains no analytics, telemetry or crash-reporting code.
  • The only service Noti's own code talks to is Google, and only if you choose to turn on Google Drive sync.
  • Locked notes are encrypted. The rest of the database is not. See "Security measures and limitations" below.

What data Noti stores

On your PC, Noti stores:

  • Your notes: title, formatted text, a plain-text copy used for search and previews, creation and modification times, pinned state, and flags for whether a note has a checklist or images.
  • Images you add to notes. These are embedded inside the note itself (as base64 data), not kept as separate files.
  • Folders, smart folders (saved searches) and tags. Tags are derived from #hashtags in your note text.
  • Notes you move to Trash. They are permanently removed after 30 days, or when you empty the Trash.
  • App settings: theme, last open note, sort order, tray / hotkey / Quick Note preferences, and the auto-lock time for locked notes.
  • If you connect Google Drive: your Google account email address and sign-in tokens (see below), plus sync bookkeeping (which note versions have been synced).
  • A diagnostic log file (noti.log in the same data folder as your notes (early startup messages may go to the Windows temp folder)) written for troubleshooting. It is local; Noti does not upload it. It records events such as note ids and sizes.

Where it is stored

By default in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Noti (for example C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Local\Noti):

  • noti.db: a SQLite database containing your notes, folders and tags
  • settings.json, ui-settings.json, shell-settings.json: preferences
  • google-account.bin: Google sign-in tokens, only if you connected Google Drive

Note: when Noti is installed as a Windows (MSIX) package, Windows may redirect these files to a package-specific location under your user profile. They still stay on your PC, but the exact path may differ. When you uninstall Noti from Windows, Windows removes this package data too, so back up or sync your notes first.

What leaves your device

Nothing leaves your device unless you turn on Google Drive sync.

Google Drive sync (optional, off by default)

If you choose "Connect" in the Sync & Backup dialog:

  • You sign in with Google in your normal web browser. Noti uses the OAuth installed-app flow with PKCE; Google redirects back to a temporary local address (127.0.0.1) on your PC. Noti never sees your Google password.
  • Noti requests only your email address (to show which account is connected) and the Google Drive drive.appdata scope. That scope gives Noti access only to an app-private, hidden folder in your Drive. Noti cannot see or change your other Drive files.
  • Noti uploads your notes, folders and smart folders to that hidden folder (one compressed file per item) and downloads changes made on your other PCs. While connected it syncs about once a minute, shortly after you edit, and on exit if edits are pending.
  • Unlocked notes are uploaded as compressed but not Noti-encrypted content, including embedded images. They are protected by your Google account, not by Noti. Locked notes are uploaded as their encrypted blob, together with non-secret metadata (dates, folder, and the title if you chose to show it).
  • Google handles that data under Google's own privacy policy. Noti's developer does not receive it.
  • Your sign-in tokens are stored on your PC in google-account.bin, encrypted with Windows DPAPI for your Windows user account.

Other network activity

Noti's editor runs in an embedded Microsoft Edge WebView2 control and loads Noti's own bundled files from a virtual local address. In the source we reviewed, the editor does not fetch remote pages, scripts or fonts. If you click a web link in a note, Noti hands it to your default browser, which then contacts that site.

We searched Noti's source for network calls. The only HTTP client in the app is used for Google (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com, www.googleapis.com). We found no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or update-check code. We cannot vouch for background traffic of Microsoft WebView2 or Windows itself, which is governed by Microsoft's policies.

What the developer collects

Nothing from the app. We do not run a server for Noti, there are no accounts, and the app does not send us your notes, usage statistics, crash reports or device identifiers. If you obtained Noti from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft may provide us with aggregate download or crash information under the Store's own terms; that does not include your notes.

Security measures and limitations

What Noti does:

  • Locked notes: each locked note is encrypted separately with AES-256-GCM using a random key. That key is wrapped by a key derived from your password with Argon2id (default 64 MiB memory, 3 iterations, parallelism 2). You can optionally create a recovery key when you lock a note.
  • When you lock a note, Noti clears its plain text from the main database, then rebuilds the database file and truncates its write-ahead log to remove leftover copies (best effort).
  • Locked notes are not added to the search index.
  • While a locked note is unlocked, its content is decrypted in the editor in memory, and its key is kept in memory encrypted with Windows DPAPI until you lock it again or the auto-lock timer (if set) expires.
  • Google sign-in tokens are encrypted with Windows DPAPI.

Honest limitations:

  • The main SQLite database (noti.db) is NOT encrypted. Anyone with access to your Windows account or disk can read your unlocked notes, titles, folder names and metadata. Use Windows BitLocker or device encryption if you need full-disk protection.
  • Locking protects note content only. A locked note's title is stored readable if you chose to show it; dates, folder and the fact that a note is locked are not encrypted.
  • If you forget a locked note's password and have no recovery key, the note cannot be recovered. We cannot reset it.
  • Unlocked notes synced to Google Drive are not encrypted by Noti.
  • Noti has not been independently security audited.

See KNOWN-LIMITATIONS.md for more detail.

Your controls and deleting your data

  • Delete a note: move it to Trash; it is removed permanently after 30 days or when you empty Trash.
  • Delete everything on this PC: uninstall Noti and delete the %LOCALAPPDATA%\Noti folder (and any package data folder Windows keeps for the app).
  • Disconnect Google Drive: Sync & Backup > Disconnect. Noti deletes the local tokens and asks Google to revoke them. Your notes stay on the PC and the backup stays in Google Drive.
  • Delete the cloud backup: in Google Drive go to Settings > Manage apps > Noti > Delete hidden app data. You can also remove Noti's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • The developer holds no copy of your data, so there is nothing for us to export or delete on your behalf.

Children

Noti is a general-purpose notes app and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.
