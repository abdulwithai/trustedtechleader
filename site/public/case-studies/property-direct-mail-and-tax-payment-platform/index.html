What it looks like in use

A mailing queue that sends letters at a steady pace, and a tax payments list where a property tax check is issued by a team member who has permission.

From a list of owners to a paid tax bill

  1. You upload your list

    You choose a spreadsheet file of land owners and the letters join a queue.

  2. Letters go out at a steady pace

    A few go out at a time, so you never send too many at once or past your daily limit.

  3. A mail service prints and posts them

    Each letter is printed and mailed for you, and the cost is billed to your account through Stripe once a day.

  4. You pay a property tax bill by check

    You find the property, and an allowed team member issues the county tax check.

  5. Sales reach your accounts

    When a property sells, the sale is recorded and copied into your accounting for you.

01

The challenge

Land and property investors send large volumes of offer letters to owners, then must track every property, closing, due-diligence step, sale and county tax payment.

In spreadsheets, that means manual mailing, scattered files, repeated re-keying into accounting and no controlled way to pay property taxes by check. Mail also has to be paced: sending too many letters at once, or outside a user's quota and paused settings, is risky. Each account may have many team members who should see only what their permissions allow.

02

The solution

The platform imports mailing data from CSV files and queues it. A scheduled job drips letters out through a print-and-mail API or uploads them to a cloud storage folder. Users manage organisations, trusts, properties, closings, due diligence, sales and tax payments from one interface, with documents such as deeds generated from templates.

Printed mail is billed through Stripe by a daily process, and tax-payment checks are issued through the same mail provider. Sales are pushed to Xero as manual journals. Account owners manage sub-users, permissions and EULA acceptance.

05

Implementation notes

Engineering decisions worth knowing about.

  1. Cron-driven pipelines

    Artisan commands handle dripping, billing, status updates, expiry emails and report mailing.

  2. Daily Stripe billing job

    Mail costs are charged by class of mail and print options, with test-mode mail exempt.

  3. Database triggers

    Triggers keep property fields in sync with mailings when mailings are deleted.

  4. Strangler-style migration

    The legacy PHP app is autoloaded alongside the Laravel app, with legacy redirects and legacy-enabled test cases.

  5. Policy-based authorisation

    Per-feature permissions, such as issuing checks, with feature flags via Laravel Pennant.

  6. Sensitive value handling

    An encryption service, a custom password hasher, session tracking and EULA gates.

  7. Layered testing

    PHPUnit feature tests, Dusk browser tests and a Codeception acceptance suite for the legacy code, with Sentry for error reporting.

Business value

What the system enables. No numbers are claimed.

  • Reduces manual mailing runs by pacing letters automatically within account limits.
  • Keeps properties, closings and sales in one place instead of scattered spreadsheets.
  • Reduces re-keying of sales into accounting by pushing journals automatically.
  • Gives account owners control over who can issue checks or access which organisation.
