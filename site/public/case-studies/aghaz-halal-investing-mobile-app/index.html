Aghaz: halal goal-based investing mobile app for US investors

A US halal investing app on Google Play: goal-based portfolios, a halal stock screener, a flat fee schedule and a regulated broker behind it.

Sector
Fintech: halal (Sharia-compliant) investing
Category
Mobile apps
Scope
One Android app, described from its public Google Play listing as of October 2026

At a glance

Who it's for
US-based Muslims who want to save for personal goals through investments that follow Islamic principles. The listing says Aghaz is for "US-based Muslims".
Core platform
An Android app on Google Play in the Finance category, published under the developer name Manzil Inc. The listing links to a website, aghazinvest.com.
What sets it apart
Investing is organised around goals (up to 10 per customer, by the listing), with portfolios the listing calls Sharia compliant, a halal stock screener, and a plain fee: 0.49% a year plus $2 a month, as listed.

01

The challenge

Many investors in the US want their money to follow their religious beliefs. For Muslim investors that means avoiding holdings that conflict with Sharia (Islamic law). Screening holdings for that takes time.

The product also sits in a regulated field. It has to say clearly who gives the advice, who holds the securities and what it costs, and it must keep to financial rules in everything it says. An app like this has to earn trust before it asks anyone to deposit money.

02

What the app does

Per the listing, Aghaz is a goals-based investment adviser in an app. A member signs up, answers a few questions about their investing style, then funds a primary account or creates a goal. For each goal, the member sets the purpose, the amount to save and the date it is due. The listing says software then builds a portfolio for that goal.

The listing says the portfolios follow Islamic principles, and that they are certified by IdealRatings (a Sharia-screening data firm) against AAOIFI standards (a body that publishes Islamic finance standards) and reviewed by the company's Sharia scholars.

03

Key features

As described in the listing's own text, October 2026.

  • 01

    Goal-based investing

    Customers can create up to 10 goals. The listing gives a first car or a house as examples, and the screenshots show template goals such as Hajj (the pilgrimage to Mecca), Vacation, Business and Marriage, plus a Custom Goal.

  • 02

    Halal portfolios

    Professionally managed, automated portfolios the listing describes as Sharia compliant. The portfolio screenshot shows holdings grouped as stocks, ETFs (exchange-traded funds), REITs (real-estate investment trusts), Sukuk (Islamic finance certificates), precious metals and cash.

  • 03

    Halal stock screener

    Members can link other investment accounts, or type in individual stocks, to check whether those holdings fit their values. The listing says access is unlimited for members and that other financial tools are included.

  • 04

    Personalised insights

    The listing says the app gives real-time insights and recommendations for each goal, so the member can see whether it remains on track.

  • 05

    Stated fee structure

    The listing states 0.49% of total investment per year plus a $2 monthly subscription fee, described as having no surprises. Fees as listed, October 2026.

06

Business value

What the setup enables.

  • Turns a values requirement (halal investing) into a clear product promise that a customer can check against named standards and parties.
  • Frames saving around life goals the customer already has, such as a car, a home, a business or Hajj, rather than around market jargon.
  • Puts the regulated structure and the fees up front in the listing, which helps a first-time investor decide whether to trust the app.
